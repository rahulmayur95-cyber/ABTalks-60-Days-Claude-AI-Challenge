Method

Each laptop's data was pulled from manufacturer spec pages and independent reviews/benchmarks (Notebookcheck, Tom's Hardware, Tom's Guide, PCWorld, Laptop Mag, Gizmodo, Fstoppers, TechRadar, Trusted Reviews, ITPro, Macworld). Where a metric was not published in any source found, the tool uses an industry-typical placeholder value and visibly flags it as an estimate — it is never silently presented as fact.

Scoring formula

For each criterion, raw values are min–max normalized across the four laptops to a 0–100 scale (higher = better; for Price and Weight the scale is inverted, since lower is better). The weighted total is the sum of (normalized score × your slider weight) divided by the sum of your weights. This means the math is fully transparent and reproducible from the numbers shown in each card — nothing is a hidden or invented composite "quality score."

Conflicts & judgment calls resolved

  • CPU/GPU performance: Geekbench 6 multi-core scores were used where an outlet published them on a comparable configuration. Apple's M4 Pro (15,234) and Dell XPS 16's Panther Lake chip (16,975) had directly cited scores. Lenovo did not publish a directly comparable Geekbench 6 multi-core number for the ThinkPad P1 in the sources found — reviewers only made relative claims (e.g. "underperforms a base 14-inch MacBook Pro with M5 by nearly 1,000 points"), so a mid-range estimate was used and flagged. ASUS ProArt's score reflects its top RTX 5090 configuration as tested by Fstoppers; lower trims will score less.
  • Battery life: Sources measure battery very differently — Apple's own rated hours vs. Notebookcheck's max-brightness web test vs. Laptop Mag's 150-nit test vs. Fstoppers' "real creative workload" test aren't directly comparable methodologies. Each laptop's most representative independent test number was used and the methodology is noted per card. Dell's XPS 16 (2026) reviews described "epic" and "long-lasting" battery life qualitatively without a specific tested hour figure in the sources found, so that value is flagged as an estimate.
  • Display quality: Combined from peak brightness (nits) and DCI-P3 color gamut coverage (%) where both were reported. Lenovo's ThinkPad P1 display metrics weren't specified with nits/gamut in the cited reviews, so this value is flagged as an estimate based on typical OLED workstation panels in this class.
  • Price: Manufacturer or lowest independently-verified starting price for a professional-grade configuration was used, not stripped-down base SKUs or maxed-out review-unit prices, to keep comparisons apples-to-apples (approximately).

Limitations

Prices, especially for Windows OEMs, fluctuate with regional sales and configuration. Benchmark scores vary by silicon bin, cooling, and OS build. This tool is a snapshot as of mid-2026 and a starting point for your own research, not a substitute for hands-on testing.
